# PPRIN: pstack's twenty-four principles, compared with Qara and csf

**Track:** pstack · **Type:** research · **Date:** 2026-11-23

**Question:** what do pstack's 24 principle skills and their inline index actually say and enforce, which of them are operational rather than slogans, where do they contradict each other and JM's standing rules, and which are worth adding to Qara or csf as a line in an existing file?

**Scope.** Every `skills/principle-*/SKILL.md` (24 files, read in full), the principles index inside `skills/poteto-mode/SKILL.md` (lines 31-72), `skills/poteto-mode/worker.md`, guide chapter `docs/guide/08-principles.md`, the README principles table, the Claude Code adapter `docs/harness/claude-code.md`, the docs checker `scripts/check-pstack-docs.mjs`, and every place another skill, playbook, agent or automation cites a principle. The playbooks, routing and wrapper agent belong to PMODE; autonomous runs and shipping to PPAR; verification and measurement skills to PPROOF; learning to PLEARN; writing and design-first to PWRITE; packaging and the generator to PPACK. They are cited here only where a principle touches them. Brainmaxxing's 16 principle files were judged one by one in NBRAIN-11; this document cites that and does not repeat it.

**Summary.**
- On the owner's removal goal (addendum, 2026-11-23): little code in the principles area can go, because the principles are the part of Qara that is already enforced by checks, and pstack's own principle 24 says a structural fix beats more text. Of the 1,705 lines of principle-enforcing checks and hooks measured, I judge 112 lines removable now (`scripts/check-typescript-exports-shape.ts`, 112 lines, a reminder of a decision's revisit trigger). `scripts/check-doc-terms.ts` stays: it is live and a commit-gate matcher (an earlier draft of this document wrongly called it a no-op). The rest should stay code or needs a read I did not finish (see "What this could replace").
- pstack ships 24 principles as 24 one-rule skills (10 to 21 lines each), plus a copy of each rule in the `poteto-mode` index, the README table and guide chapter 8. Fifteen are the principles from brainmaxxing (NBRAIN-11), one of them renamed; nine are new.
- The leaf files are never loaded by the platform: every one carries `disable-model-invocation: true`, and the Claude Code adapter says the Skill tool refuses most pstack skills. A leaf is read only because a prose line in `poteto-mode` or `poteto-agent` tells the agent to read it. Qara measured exactly that kind of pointer at 0 of 4 loads (`.claude/skills/code-style/SKILL.md:54-58`). The only script check on the principles is the count and the links.
- About half are operational (they name a trigger and a check you can run): 12 of 24, plus 8 partial and 4 that are slogans. The best are `explain-the-number`, `test-behavior-not-implementation`, `attack-the-premise`, `fix-root-causes`, `sequence-verifiable-units` and `make-operations-idempotent`.
- pstack has no precedence rule between principles. The conflicts are patched one at a time inside playbooks. `never-block-on-the-human` is stated three different ways in three files, and `worker.md` bans asking a person at all.
- Qara already holds 14 of the 24 as stated or stronger. Six are held weaker, two are held in the opposite form (`outcome-oriented-execution` against the commit gate, `never-block-on-the-human` against W-1 and the acts table), and two are missing (`explain-the-number`, `experience-first`).
- Worth adding: four short clauses to existing Qara files (the shared-premise step of `attack-the-premise`, the legitimacy test of `fix-root-causes`, two assertion rules from `test-behavior-not-implementation`, the restart test of `make-operations-idempotent`), and `explain-the-number` handed to PPROOF. Not worth copying: the structure of 24 leaf files with an inline index, the "name each principle in your reply" rule, and the two principles that conflict with Qara's gates.

## Doctrines

**PPRIN-1. Count the 24 as 15 inherited from brainmaxxing plus 9 new, and judge only the 9 new ones afresh.**
Fifteen pstack principles are the brainmaxxing set judged in NBRAIN-11, with one rename (`serialize-shared-state-mutations` became `separate-before-serializing-shared-state`) and with `cost-aware-delegation` dropped. The nine new ones are `laziness-protocol`, `attack-the-premise`, `minimize-reader-load`, `build-the-lever`, `model-the-domain`, `type-system-discipline`, `sequence-verifiable-units`, `test-behavior-not-implementation` and `explain-the-number`.
- Why: it avoids re-arguing NBRAIN-11, and it shows where the author has been adding since: five of the nine new ones are about verification or proof (`attack-the-premise`, `sequence-verifiable-units`, `test-behavior-not-implementation`, `explain-the-number`, `build-the-lever` as "the tool that proves it"), none about product or planning.
- Evidence: the 24 directory names under `pstack/skills/principle-*`; the 16 names in `noodle/brain.md:94-97` and the brainmaxxing list at `noodle/brain.md:141,170-171`. The groups are Core 10, Architecture 6, Verification 5, Delegation 2, Meta 1 (`pstack/skills/poteto-mode/SKILL.md:35-72`, `pstack/docs/guide/08-principles.md:29-68`). The author also says the leaves are "10 to 26 lines each" in brainmaxxing; pstack's are 9 to 21 lines (`wc -l`), so `exhaust-the-design-space` at 9 lines is the shortest.
- Basis: Verified (names, counts, groups); Unverified (that the nine were added after brainmaxxing; the clone has no history).
- Code: none; reference table below.

**PPRIN-2. The design is one rule copied into five places, and only the count of copies is checked by a script.**
Each principle's trigger and rule appears in the leaf's frontmatter `description`, the leaf body, the `poteto-mode` index entry, the README row, and the guide chapter bullet. The wording differs between copies. `scripts/check-pstack-docs.mjs` checks that README claims of "24 principles" match the tree, that every skill appears in the README, that relative links resolve and that slash commands exist. It does not compare the index to the leaves.
- Why: five copies drift. A reader who relies on the index gets a different trigger than one who reads the leaf. Example: the index for `Prove It Works` is "After a task, before declaring done. Verify against the real artifact, not a proxy or 'it compiles'" while the leaf says "after completing a task, before declaring done" and adds "run the feature, read the actual value, inspect the diff" (`pstack/skills/poteto-mode/SKILL.md:59`, `pstack/skills/principle-prove-it-works/SKILL.md:3`).
- Evidence: the README states the intent: "the standalone files are there so other skills can reference a principle by name, and so the index can point at the full rule for each" (`pstack/README.md:224`). The checker: `CLAIM` regex `scripts/check-pstack-docs.mjs:24`, expected counts `:76-81`, README listing `:127-129`; it reads only `README.md` and `docs/guide/*.md` (`:43-44`), never `poteto-mode/SKILL.md` or a leaf.
- Basis: Verified
- Code: Gap: nothing compares index text to leaf descriptions. A one-line check would do it; not needed for Qara (see PPRIN-13).

**PPRIN-3. The standalone leaves are not auto-loaded; they are read only if an agent obeys a prose pointer, and the pointer is the weakest form of loading Qara has measured.**
All 24 leaves set `disable-model-invocation: true`, so the platform never injects them and never lists them to the model. `poteto-mode` tells the agent "Read the leaf skill in full for any principle you apply" and "Cite only principles whose leaf SKILL.md you read this session". `poteto-agent` tells a delegate to "Navigate to a leaf `principle-*` skill whenever you apply that principle". In Claude Code the adapter says: to run another pstack skill, `Read` its `SKILL.md`; "The `Skill` tool refuses most pstack skills".
- Why: the index entry is therefore the only text guaranteed to be in context, and it is shorter than the leaf (PPRIN-5). The design assumes the agent notices that a principle applies, then spends a tool call to read a file it was told about in prose.
- Evidence: `pstack/skills/principle-*/SKILL.md:4` (flag, all 24); `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/SKILL.md:15,33`; `pstack/agents/poteto-agent.md:10`; `pstack/docs/harness/claude-code.md:12`; the generated copy keeps the flag (`harness/pstack/claude-code/skills/principle-prove-it-works/SKILL.md:4`). Qara's own measurement: "a prose 'read this file' pointer measured 0/4 across placements and models (2026-09-06 probes)", and the fix it chose, `skills:` preload for the two engineer agents (`.claude/skills/code-style/SKILL.md:54-62` as read from the preloaded text, "How this loads"). pstack's own eval playbook refuses to grade by self-report: "Don't ask the candidate to list which skills, principles, or files they applied ... grade chain-following from code shape, not self-report" (`pstack/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/eval.md:9`).
- Basis: Verified (flags, pointers, adapter text, Qara's recorded probe); Unverified (that pstack's pointer fails in practice; no probe of its own is published in the clone).
- Code: none in pstack. Gap: no check that a leaf was read.

**PPRIN-4. Sort the principles by one test: does it name a trigger and a check that someone could run? Twelve do, eight half do, four do not.**
The criterion: operational means the text gives (a) a situation that triggers it and (b) a test, count, artifact or sequence whose result can be observed without trusting the agent's word. Partial means a trigger but only values or directions. Slogan means neither beyond the title.
- Why: a slogan costs context on every read and changes nothing a reviewer can check. The operational ones can be turned into a line in a gate or a brief; the others cannot.
- Evidence: the classification column in the reference table, from the leaf texts. Strongest: `explain-the-number` (write the decision and noise floor first, name the limiter "from a profile or counters taken during a run", "keep the run count, the spread, and the limiter with the number", `pstack/skills/principle-explain-the-number/SKILL.md:9-12`); `test-behavior-not-implementation` (break the behavior, change the internals, keep what catches the first and survives the second, `:11-16`); `make-operations-idempotent` ("What happens if this runs twice? What if the previous run crashed at every possible point?", `:15`); `minimize-reader-load` (30-second test, `:15`); `build-the-lever` ("No script, codemod, generator, or delegate skill in the diff means you didn't apply it", `:13`). Weakest: `laziness-protocol` ("Make the smallest change that solves the problem. Fewer lines beat 'elegant' boilerplate", `:10-11`), `guard-the-context-window` (one bullet, "Limit files per phase, set turn budgets", no number, `:12`), `experience-first` ("choose delight"), `exhaust-the-design-space` (one sentence in the body, `:9`).
- Basis: Verified (texts); Unverified (the classification is my judgement).
- Code: none.

**PPRIN-5. Five leaves do not state their own rule in the body, so the rule lives only in a frontmatter description that no catalog shows.**
`exhaust-the-design-space` has one body sentence ("It also applies to product design decisions where user experience depends on feel, not logic"); the rule "build 2-3 competing prototypes" is only in its description. `encode-lessons-in-structure`'s body is the fallback case and a corollary; the main rule (encode as lint, flag, check or script) is in the description. `migrate-callers-then-delete-legacy-apis` has one bullet about tests and three "when this applies" conditions; "migrate callers and delete the old API in the same wave" is only in the description. `guard-the-context-window` has a Why and one bullet. `laziness-protocol` states its rule in two bullets and is not counted.
- Why: a reader who opens the file sees the frontmatter, so the file is complete for them. The risk is the in-between reader: the one who gets the index entry only (PPRIN-3). For these four, the index entry is then the whole rule.

- Basis: Verified

**PPRIN-6. "Name each principle that shaped a decision" is a self-report rule, and pstack's own guide half admits it.**
`poteto-mode` requires: "In your reply, name each principle that shaped a decision and the specific choice it changed." The guide adds: "A principle citation with no decision behind it means the agent named the rule without applying it." The eval playbook then refuses to grade by self-report.
- Why: citation is cheap to produce and cannot be checked. Qara's checks measure artifacts (a failing test, a diff, an exit code), not announcements. Copying the citation rule would add reply length and nothing to verify.
- Evidence: `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/SKILL.md:15`; `pstack/docs/guide/08-principles.md:27`; `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/eval.md:9`. The author's own use of names is as steering vocabulary: "one phrase redirects the work more precisely than a paragraph" (`pstack/docs/guide/08-principles.md:5`).
- Basis: Verified (texts); Unverified (the effect of the rule on agent behaviour).
- Code: none. Qara contrast: `CLAUDE.md:38` "Ground claims in exact source or command output."

**PPRIN-7. pstack has no rule for resolving a conflict between principles; it patches individual conflicts inside the playbook or skill that meets them.**
A search of the index, the leaves, the guide chapter and the playbooks for precedence wording ("precedence", "outrank", "takes priority", "overrides the") finds none that ranks principles. What it finds are four point fixes: `feature.md` says "Laziness Protocol does not override" mandatory delegation; `no-comments` says `fix-root-causes` and `redesign-from-first-principles` "guide intent only", neither "authorizes widening the fence"; `prototype.md` says it is "the one playbook where the Laziness Protocol's 'smallest change' and the verification bar invert"; `hillclimb.md` says "Correctness and simplicity outrank the number".
- Why: each pair below is a real conflict an agent will meet, and the agent decides by judgement. For Qara this is the argument against importing the set wholesale: Qara's gates (commit gate, one question per turn, acts table) already settle these, and a second unranked rulebook would reopen them.
- Evidence: `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/feature.md:12`; `pstack/skills/no-comments/SKILL.md:22`; `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/prototype.md:5`; `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/hillclimb.md:17`. Conflict pairs found: see the conflict table in Reference.
- Basis: Verified (the searches and the four fixes); Unverified (that an unranked set produces wrong choices in practice).
- Code: none. Gap: no precedence list.

**PPRIN-8. `never-block-on-the-human` is stated three ways, and the strictest wording bans a question that JM's own rules require.**
The leaf says reversible work proceeds and irreversible actions "(force-push, delete production data, send external messages) still require confirmation". The `poteto-mode` Autonomy section says "Reversible work and external actions (team chat, ticket updates, kicking off evals) proceed without asking" and "Always pause" only for force-push to shared branches, deploys, data deletion, customer messages; then "Session overrides: 'Don't stop' / 'going to bed' / 'run until done' / 'be fully autonomous' -> keep going." `worker.md`, which every delegated agent reads first, says: "Never call a tool that shows a person a question. Make any choice you can undo, then say what you chose and why. Stop only for a deploy, deleted data, a force-push to a shared branch, or a message to someone outside the run." The autonomous-run playbook adds "Do not park reversible work for the human or use `AskQuestion`."
- Why: "send external messages" needs confirmation in the leaf, but "team chat" proceeds in the index. The worker rule bans the question tool even where the stop rule fires. This conflicts with W-1 (each question is its own prompt through the question UI, asked only when it cannot be settled), with Qara's E1 (`AskUserQuestion` allowed for unresolved intent or explicit permission, `delegation-guide.md:142`), and with the standing rule that outward-facing acts need JM. Qara and JM have already adopted the safe half: "Resolve facts through investigation, choose reversible defaults, and fix ordinary failures without asking" (`delegation-guide.md:54-58,64-65`). csf is stricter the other way: "do not guess past an ambiguity; surface it instead" (`csf-build .../stage-briefs/assembler.md:189-190`).
- Evidence: `pstack/skills/principle-never-block-on-the-human/SKILL.md:13-16`; `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/SKILL.md:74-82`; `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/worker.md:7`; `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/autonomous-run.md:9`; `pstack/skills/figure-it-out/SKILL.md:23` ("a multi-hour run earns one checkpoint"); `DOCTRINES.md` W-1; `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` (Tool Discipline, one question per AskUserQuestion call); `.claude/context/acts.md:24` (csf staging: no asking for database writes, deploys, builds; credentials, sudo, force-push and gate bypass stay excluded), `:9` (force-push never).
- Basis: Verified (texts); Unverified (the net effect on JM's workflow).
- Code: none. Gap: none to fill in Qara; do not import the principle (see PPRIN-14).

**PPRIN-9. `outcome-oriented-execution` and `migrate-callers-then-delete-legacy-apis` assume a project with no gate at every commit and no external consumers, which neither Qara nor csf is.**
Outcome-oriented says "Intermediate breakage is acceptable when it is planned, scoped, and reversible" and "Require full static and runtime verification at plan completion". `sequence-verifiable-units` says "do not advance until the current one is green". The refactoring playbook says both in adjacent steps: small steps "each keeping the pin green" and "migrate every caller and delete the old API in the same wave ... No compatibility shims". Migrate-callers states its own condition: "No external users depend on backward compatibility."
- Why: Qara's commit gate is a hard stop: "Treat the commit gate's verdict as authority: diagnose a block, never bypass or weaken it" (`CLAUDE.md:43`). A planned-breakage phase cannot be committed on a gated branch, so the principle collapses into "do the whole wave in one commit", which is what migrate-callers asks anyway. csf-build is a library consumed by cub, so "no external users" is false there: its own reviewer keeps `critical_count` "for backward compatibility" (`reviewer-agent/orchestration-process.md:102`). Qara already enforces the consumer half where it matters: provider removals name their consumers and the reviewer runs each consumer's typecheck against the PR head (`delegation-guide.md:172`).
- Evidence: `pstack/skills/principle-outcome-oriented-execution/SKILL.md:9-21`; `pstack/skills/principle-migrate-callers-then-delete-legacy-apis/SKILL.md:11-14`; `pstack/skills/principle-sequence-verifiable-units/SKILL.md:9`; `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/refactoring.md:11`; the Qara and csf lines above. pstack resolves the tension only with the guardrails inside outcome-oriented ("Declare where temporary breakage is acceptable").
- Basis: Verified (texts); Unverified (that a Qara plan has ever needed planned breakage).
- Code: none. Gap: none; leave both out of Qara's principle set.

**PPRIN-11. Four clauses are worth adding to existing Qara files; none needs a new file.**
(1) From `attack-the-premise`: when the same failure repeats, write down the one premise the failed attempts shared and test it before the next attempt. (2) From `fix-root-causes`: a guard is a fix only when the state it handles is legitimate; fix the pattern, not the instance. (3) From `test-behavior-not-implementation`: derive the expected value from the requirement, never from the code under test; assert the error type callers branch on, not the message; pair "this never happens" with a case where it does. (4) From `make-operations-idempotent`: the restart test, "what if the previous run crashed at every possible point", with leftovers recognisable as stale by evidence that holds wherever the run stopped.
- Why: each fills a measured hole. Qara's current text says "After the same failure repeats, change the approach; after the third failure on one subtask, stop and surface the evidence" (`CLAUDE.md:37`) and gives hypotheses a place in the brief ("Plausible root causes become hypotheses to discriminate with evidence", `worker-common.md:69`), but never says to find the shared premise. The testing guide says "Tests verify behavior through public interfaces" (`testing-guide.md:69`) but has none of the three assertion rules; a search of `docs/TESTING.md`, `testing-guide.md` and `tdd-qa/` for "error message", "expected value", "computed by", "never happens" found nothing. Idempotency appears in Qara only as a CLI property ("Same command = same result", `cli-first-guide.md:63-74`), not as a crash-recovery test for hooks and state files, the area where Qara has had stale-state incidents (`tdd-cycle.md:5` retells one).
- Evidence: `pstack/skills/principle-attack-the-premise/SKILL.md:9-15`; `principle-fix-root-causes/SKILL.md:9-14`; `principle-test-behavior-not-implementation/SKILL.md:11-16`; `principle-make-operations-idempotent/SKILL.md:9-15`; the Qara lines above.
- Basis: Verified (what each source says and what Qara lacks, by the searches named); Unverified (that each clause changes behaviour).
- Code: Gap: see Borrow list for targets and size.

**PPRIN-12. `explain-the-number` is the strongest new principle and has no Qara counterpart; it belongs in the PPROOF decision, not here.**
It requires: write the decision and the result that would change it before running; name the limiter from a profile; list what else the number could be measuring (errors, skipped or cached work, code that never ran, an untuned side, noise) and rule each out; keep run count, spread and limiter with the number. It points to a `benchmark-checklist` skill for performance and to three questions for evals.
- Why: Qara's rule is general: "Ground claims in exact source or command output. Do not infer success from silence" (`CLAUDE.md:38`). It has no measurement-specific rule. Qara has quoted numbers with their measurement conditions in places ("360 s cold, 0.2 s from a warm cache, measured 2026-10-20", `delegation-guide.md:181`), but as a habit, not a stated check. A search of Qara skills found no `benchmark*` skill directory.
- Evidence: `pstack/skills/principle-explain-the-number/SKILL.md:9-14`; `pstack/docs/guide/06-verify-and-ship.md:49`; `pstack/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/hillclimb.md:8` (cites `benchmark-checklist`, not the principle). Cross-reference: PPROOF.
- Basis: Verified (text; absence of a Qara skill by directory search); Unverified (that Qara has a measurement problem that needs it).
- Code: Gap: PPROOF owns the decision.

**PPRIN-13. Do not copy "one principle per small file with an inline index"; Qara already has the same two-part design in two files, and a leaf layer would add copies without adding loading.**
Qara's `MISSION.md` states the nine principles (canonical, always loaded) and `PRINCIPLES.md` gives each one a gate-function test, with a drift rule: "the numbering below mirrors `MISSION.md` § Principles exactly ... a principle with no matching test (or vice versa) is a drift bug" (`PRINCIPLES.md:13-15`; `MISSION.md:34-51`). That is the index-plus-test design, in two files and 187 lines. pstack's variant adds a third and fourth copy and loads neither.
- Why: the benefit pstack claims is that other skills can cite a principle by name. That works equally with a heading anchor in `PRINCIPLES.md`. The cost is five copies (PPRIN-2) and leaves that are hidden from the platform (PPRIN-3). The one transferable form detail is the "Apply when ..." first clause that names the trigger; Qara's gates already open with "Gate (before ...)" (`PRINCIPLES.md:31-32,43-44`), which is the same job.
- Evidence: `PRINCIPLES.md:13-15,19-110`; `MISSION.md:34-51`; pstack evidence in PPRIN-2 and PPRIN-3. The decision on the form of the Qara tests: `decisions/2026-06-27-structural-scaffolding-sanctioned.md` as cited at `PRINCIPLES.md:7-11` (not opened).
- Basis: Verified (Qara files); Unverified (that a leaf layer would not help Qara's other harnesses).
- Code: none.

**PPRIN-14. Do not import the principle set as a block, and never as written for agents; it embeds instructions that cut across JM's standing rules.**
Embedded agent instructions in the principle layer, listed for the owner:
